Chemistry reels make the invisible visible and tie every claim to particles, equations and numbers.
- For equilibrium, gas behaviour, pressure, concentration, temperature and rate topics, ALWAYS use the `particle_lab` prefab: a glass cylinder with a plunger, coloured particles and a pressure gauge. The particle counts come from an equilibrium solver (`eqext`, `Keq`, `vanthoff`), so they obey Le Chatelier's principle exactly: you choose the reaction (`a`, `b`, `c`, `d` for a A + b B <=> c C + d D, 0 = absent), the reference counts and what changes (`vol`, `dA`..`dD`, `Kt`, `spd`), never the counts themselves. Never fake a shift with your own particle expressions. Worked setups to adapt:

- The lab must show the reaction you are narrating: set `a`..`d` to its coefficients, `cA`..`cD` to distinct, saturated colours that stay readable on the look (never near-white on a light look) and `lA`..`lD` to the species formulas for the legend (Unicode subscripts, e.g. "NO₂"). Colours and formulas are fixed for the whole reel: use the same `id` and the same reaction, counts, colours and legend in every scene, and change only `vol`, `dA`..`dD`, `Kt` and `spd` (the renderer enforces this). The default N₂/H₂/NH₃ setup is for ammonia only.
- A pressure or volume change moves only `vol`; the solver does the shifting. Use `dA`..`dD` only to add or remove a substance the narration says is added or removed, never to show the shift itself.
- Say what the picture shows in the order it happens: the disturbance, then the system's response, then the new state. The direction of every shift must match the principle: more moles of gas -> lower pressure favoured by expansion; adding a reactant -> forward; heating -> toward the endothermic direction.
- Use `molecule_h2o`, `molecule_nh3`, `molecule_ch4`, `molecule_co2`, `molecule_diatomic` and `molecule_triatomic` (true bond lengths and angles) when the beat is about a molecule's shape, with `tag` pins naming the atoms. Each molecule you show must be the one you name: NO and O₂ are `molecule_diatomic`, NO₂ is `molecule_triatomic` (angle 134, centre N blue, outer O red), SO₂ is triatomic (119), and never use the water or CO₂ prefab for another substance. For a reaction shown as molecules, place reactants and products with the same colours as the lab legend.
- Write every chemical formula and equation in a `formula` card with `\ce{...}`, for example `\ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) <=> 2NH3(g)}`, with state symbols and a reversible arrow `<=>` for an equilibrium. Never show an unbalanced equation; every equation you speak or show must be balanced.
- Use `graph` for concentration against time or any rate relationship, and `derivation` for K expressions and worked calculations; quote only the numbers in the narration.
- Keep the lab in the middle of the window: with a formula card at the top anchor, aim the camera so the cylinder sits below the card (camera target y about 2.0 at distance 7 to 8).
- The narrator stands in a lower corner; keep the cylinder and gauge out of that corner (place the lab slightly left of centre).
